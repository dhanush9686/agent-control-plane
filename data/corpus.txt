=== KGF: Chapter 1 (2018) ===
Director: Prashanth Neel. Language: Kannada, released across multiple dubbed versions.
Setting: the Kolar Gold Fields (KGF), a brutal, privately-controlled gold mining region run through forced labor.

Plot: Rocky is born into poverty in Mumbai's slums. After his mother dies, having made him promise as a child that he would become the most powerful and wealthiest man alive, he rises through the city's criminal underworld through sheer ruthlessness and cunning. A crime boss, Shivanna, sends Rocky to seize control of KGF and eliminate its violent overseer, Garuda, who has ruled the mine and its enslaved workers with total brutality for decades. Rocky infiltrates KGF, gradually wins the loyalty of the laborers by defying Garuda's regime, and in a final confrontation kills Garuda, taking control of the gold fields for himself. The film ends with Rocky now the secret, unchallenged ruler of KGF's gold wealth.

Key characters: Rocky (protagonist), Garuda (antagonist, former ruler of KGF), Shivanna (Mumbai crime boss who sends Rocky).

=== KGF: Chapter 2 (2022) ===
Direct sequel to KGF: Chapter 1, same director.

Plot: Rocky now secretly controls KGF and its gold output, disguising the operation as a legitimate mining company. His hold is threatened on two fronts: Adheera, a scarred, near-mythic warlord who served the mine's original owners and wants revenge and control back, and Ramika Sen, a corrupt and ruthless Prime Minister who wants to expose and seize the illegal gold operation for political gain. A journalist, framed as the narrator piecing Rocky's story together after the fact, drives much of the film's structure. Rocky defeats Adheera in a large-scale final battle, but the film closes with Rocky's own death, orchestrated by the political forces that could not defeat him by force.

Key characters: Rocky, Adheera (rival warlord), Ramika Sen (Prime Minister and political antagonist), the unnamed journalist narrating the story in flashback.

=== Salaar: Part 1 – Ceasefire (2023) ===
Director: Prashanth Neel. Language: Telugu.
Setting: Khansaar, a fictional, dystopian city-state run not by a single government but by a fractured council of tribal warlord factions under a fragile, ceremonial monarchy.

Plot: Deva and Vardharaja ("Varadha") grow up as inseparable childhood friends until a violent incident tears their families apart and separates them. Years later, Varadha is next in line to inherit control of Khansaar, but a brutal succession war breaks out among the city's rival factions as rulership becomes contested. Deva, who has deliberately kept away from Khansaar and its politics, is pulled back in when Varadha calls on him in a moment of crisis. Much of the film traces Deva's transformation from a reluctant outsider into Varadha's fiercely loyal, devastatingly effective protector. The film ends on a cliffhanger mid-conflict, explicitly setting up a second part.

Key characters: Deva (protagonist), Vardharaja "Varadha" (Deva's childhood friend, heir to Khansaar), several unnamed Khansaar faction leaders.

=== Dragon (Prashanth Neel, unreleased as of September 2026) ===
Status: not yet released. Currently slated for a June 2027 release, after facing earlier delays from its originally planned 2026 dates. Because it has not released, plot detail below is limited to what has been publicly confirmed — this entry is intentionally thinner than the others.

Confirmed so far: directed by Prashanth Neel, starring Jr NTR as a character named Luger, an assassin connected to a colonial-era opium-smuggling syndicate. The story is framed around a premise where British colonial control of India was substantially sustained by dominance of the opium trade. Supporting cast includes Anil Kapoor, Biju Menon, and Rukmini Vasanth. Ravi Basrur, who scored both KGF films and Salaar, also composes Dragon's music. Neel has described it as a shift in visual style from his earlier work, with more international shooting locations.

Universe note: despite fan speculation, Prashanth Neel has explicitly and publicly stated that Dragon has no direct narrative connection to KGF or Salaar — no shared characters, setting, or continuity. He has instead described it as the concluding film of his "mass-action trilogy" in tone and directorial style, not in shared story-world.

=== Universe notes ===
KGF and Salaar are not set in the same continuity. KGF takes place around the Kolar Gold Fields; Salaar takes place in the entirely separate fictional city-state of Khansaar. There is no canonical crossover or shared timeline between them. What connects the three projects (KGF, Salaar, Dragon) is Prashanth Neel as director and, for KGF and Salaar, composer Ravi Basrur — not a shared narrative universe. Any claim that these films form one connected continuity is a fan/media narrative, not something confirmed by the filmmaker.

=== Inception (2010) ===
Director: Christopher Nolan. Starring Leonardo DiCaprio as Dom Cobb, with Joseph Gordon-Levitt (Arthur), Elliot Page (Ariadne), Tom Hardy (Eames), Ken Watanabe (Saito), Cillian Murphy (Robert Fischer) and Marion Cotillard (Mal). Music by Hans Zimmer.

Plot: Cobb is a skilled thief who steals secrets from people's minds by entering their dreams. Saito offers him a way back to his children: perform "inception", planting an idea in the mind of Robert Fischer, heir to a rival energy conglomerate. Cobb assembles a team and descends through several nested dream levels. His late wife Mal, a projection from his guilt, repeatedly sabotages the mission. The film ends with Cobb spinning his totem, a top, and cutting to black before it is shown whether it falls, leaving the ending ambiguous.

Awards: Inception won four Academy Awards: Best Visual Effects, Best Cinematography, Best Sound Editing and Best Sound Mixing.

=== Interstellar (2014) ===
Director: Christopher Nolan. Starring Matthew McConaughey as Cooper, Anne Hathaway as Brand, Jessica Chastain as the adult Murph, and Michael Caine as Professor Brand. Music by Hans Zimmer. Physicist Kip Thorne was a scientific consultant.

Plot: A blight is destroying Earth's crops. Cooper, a former pilot turned farmer, joins a secret NASA mission through a wormhole near Saturn to find a habitable planet for humanity. Because of the black hole Gargantua, time passes far more slowly for the crew than on Earth, so his daughter Murph grows older than him. On one planet the scientist Mann, whom the crew had revered, betrays them. Cooper falls into the black hole and finds a tesseract through which he sends gravitational signals to young Murph. She uses them to solve the equation that lets humanity leave Earth. Cooper wakes near Saturn and heads to join Brand.

Awards: Interstellar won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects.

=== The Dark Knight (2008) ===
Director: Christopher Nolan. Christian Bale plays Batman, Heath Ledger the Joker, Aaron Eckhart Harvey Dent, Gary Oldman Commissioner Gordon, and Maggie Gyllenhaal Rachel Dawes. Music by Hans Zimmer and James Newton Howard. It is the second film of Nolan's Batman trilogy, after Batman Begins (2005) and before The Dark Knight Rises (2012), and the three share one continuity.

Plot: The Joker, an anarchic criminal, terrorizes Gotham to force Batman to reveal his identity. He engineers a situation in which Rachel Dawes dies and Harvey Dent is horribly scarred, turning Dent into Two-Face. After Dent dies, Batman takes the blame for Dent's crimes so that the city can keep believing in its "white knight".

Awards: Heath Ledger won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor posthumously for playing the Joker.

=== Oppenheimer (2023) ===
Director: Christopher Nolan. Cillian Murphy plays J. Robert Oppenheimer and Robert Downey Jr. plays Lewis Strauss. The film is based on the book American Prometheus by Kai Bird and Martin J. Sherwin. Music by Ludwig Goransson.

Plot: The film follows Oppenheimer as scientific director of the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos, which builds the first atomic bomb, culminating in the Trinity test. It then follows his 1954 security hearing, in which his clearance is stripped, and the later Senate confirmation hearing of Strauss, who had worked against him.

Awards: Oppenheimer won seven Academy Awards at the 2024 ceremony, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor for Murphy and Best Supporting Actor for Downey Jr.

=== Memento (2000) ===
Director: Christopher Nolan, based on the short story "Memento Mori" by his brother Jonathan Nolan. Guy Pearce plays Leonard Shelby, Carrie-Anne Moss plays Natalie and Joe Pantoliano plays Teddy.

Plot: Leonard has anterograde amnesia and cannot form new memories. He hunts the man who attacked his wife, relying on Polaroid photographs, handwritten notes and tattoos. The film alternates color sequences, shown in reverse chronological order, with black-and-white sequences shown in forward order, so the viewer shares Leonard's confusion.

=== Dunkirk (2017) ===
Director: Christopher Nolan. Cast includes Fionn Whitehead, Tom Hardy as the Spitfire pilot Farrier, and Mark Rylance as Mr. Dawson, a civilian boat owner. Music by Hans Zimmer.

Plot: The film depicts the 1940 evacuation of Allied soldiers from the beaches of Dunkirk. It interweaves three timelines: one week on the land, one day at sea, and one hour in the air.

=== Tenet (2020, entry intentionally thin) ===
This entry is intentionally limited. Director: Christopher Nolan. John David Washington plays the lead, known only as the Protagonist. The film's central idea is the "inversion" of time. No further cast, crew or plot detail is recorded in this knowledge base.

=== Universe notes: Nolan ===
Only the three Batman films (Batman Begins, The Dark Knight, The Dark Knight Rises) share a continuity. Inception, Interstellar, Memento, Dunkirk, Oppenheimer and Tenet are standalone stories with no shared setting or characters. What links them is the director, and for some of them the composer Hans Zimmer and the actors Cillian Murphy, Michael Caine and Tom Hardy appearing across films, not a shared universe.
